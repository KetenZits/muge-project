## Overall assessment

**23/40 — Acceptable; significant usability improvements needed. No P0 blocker was established.** This is a heuristic design-review score, not a measured user-success rate.

MU Connect has a coherent, welcoming visual foundation. Its collaboration data is more distinctive than its composition: roles, skills, availability, deadlines, and competition-to-team relationships are useful, while repeated promotional headers and similar card layouts could belong to many community products. ## Detailed findings

### MU-01 · P1 · Core mobile destinations and personal navigation are hard to reconcile

**Pages:** Navigation, Home, Team Finder, Communities, Competitions, Profile. **Affected components:** Sidebar, Topbar, MobileNav. **Evidence:** Code + saved captures.

Desktop exposes ten destinations. Mobile exposes Home, Discover, Alerts, and “Profile,” plus Create; “Profile” opens /me, which desktop calls My Activity. Team Finder, Messages, Communities, Competitions, and Saved have no persistent mobile destination. The actual profile uses another route.

**Impact:** The primary collaboration task becomes harder to find on a phone, and students must learn two names for the same personal area.

**Recommended improvement:** Keep the shell and introduce a clearly labeled route to the remaining destinations. Prioritize Team Finder and Messages in that decision. Align Profile/My Activity naming, destination, and active states across device sizes.

**Acceptance check for a later implementation:** At 375, 768, and 1024px, a newcomer can locate Team Finder, Messages, and their actual profile through persistent, labeled navigation.

**Source:** [Desktop navigation](/C:/Users/User/Documents/GitHub/muge-project/mu-commu-hub/src/components/layout/navigation.tsx:26); [Mobile navigation](/C:/Users/User/Documents/GitHub/muge-project/mu-commu-hub/src/components/layout/navigation.tsx:247).

### MU-02 · P1 · Search links and “View all” discard the student's intent

**Pages:** Discover, Navigation. **Affected components:** DiscoverView, RightRail, SectionHead, SearchPalette. **Evidence:** Code.

Topic links send /discover?q=..., but Discover initializes an empty query and does not read it. Section links open unfiltered directories. Discover truncates collections into previews, while global search also restricts some collections; continuing elsewhere can require rebuilding the original search.

**Impact:** A relevant topic click can appear to do nothing useful, and a student loses the context that made a result worth opening.

**Recommended improvement:** Initialize and preserve applicable query, category, and filter state in URLs/navigation. Carry it through “View all,” show when a list is a preview, and align the global search promise with its actual coverage.

**Acceptance check for a later implementation:** A #AI topic click visibly opens AI results; expanding a filtered section preserves its applicable search and explains any scope change.

### MU-03 · P1 · A selected result category can be blank without an explanation

**Pages:** Discover. **Affected components:** DiscoverView result sections and EmptyState. **Evidence:** Code.

## Page priorities and visual distinction

- **Home:** reduce repeated compose prompts and generic introduction space; make the feed and a useful route to teams clearer. Keep the bounded gallery and actionable recruitment context. See MU-04, MU-08, MU-09, MU-16.
- **Discover:** make it an intent-led exploration surface with context-aware filters, selected-scope counts, a reliable empty state, and preserved search. See MU-02, MU-03, MU-05, MU-07.
- **Team Finder:** make open roles, fit, deadline, and the join/recruit jobs its main comparison structure. Keep every existing filter and card fact, but disclose secondary information by context. See MU-07, MU-09, MU-10, MU-18.
- **Competitions:** emphasize deadlines, status, eligibility, team size, and the path to teammates. Keep featured content consistent with active filtering. See MU-09, MU-11.
- **Communities:** lead with subject and existing discussion context, with clear local membership semantics. Do not fabricate activity or verified membership data. See MU-06, MU-12.
- **Profile:** lead with identity, skills, availability, shared fit, and contact; remove redundant biography/skill framing and preserve browsing context. See MU-13, MU-15, MU-16.

Shared brand consistency is a strength. Page distinction should come from the decision each page supports and the order/density of its content. Subtle semantic accents can reinforce that structure; assigning a different arbitrary palette to every page would add noise. Repeated cards are not automatically defects: retain them where a bounded entity or comparison benefits, and reduce borders/nesting that only repeat an existing grouping.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

The supplied checklist identifies **three failed items, a moderate structural cognitive-load risk** on the busiest discovery/recruitment surfaces: undifferentiated filter chunking, too many refinements exposed together, and absent progressive disclosure. Grouping is clear; filters are optional; headings and repeated decision facts provide useful recognition. More than four visible choices is a review prompt, not evidence that a user is incapable of handling the screen.

Keep control groups separate when interpreting counts: desktop navigation has ten destinations; Discover has seven scopes and eight refinements; Team Finder has six refinements plus sort; Home and Competitions each have four view/status choices; Communities has one search; Profile has seven section choices, with one panel disclosed at a time. The priority is relevance, hierarchy, and continuity, rather than deleting useful choices to meet a number.

The opening feels welcoming. The likely positive peak is finding a team whose missing role matches a student's skills. The likely friction is locating that task, processing filters and repeated metadata, then rebuilding context after navigation. The commitment moment needs an honest answer to “What happened, and what next?” These are walkthrough hypotheses, not observations from participants.
